  <h3>Glicko-2 MP-Weighted: Multiplayer Weighting &amp; Fractional Game Variance</h3>
  <div>

      <p>
    In competitive Through the Ages, matches are routinely contested with 2, 3, or 4 players. When a 4-player game finishes, decomposing it into pairwise outcomes generates $\binom{4}{2} = 6$ pairwise comparisons ($A\gt B, A\gt C, A\gt D, B\gt C, B\gt D, C\gt D$).
  </p>
  <p>
    In naive pairwise rating systems (like Standard Glicko), each of these 6 pairs is treated as an <strong>independent 1v1 duel with full weight $w = 1.0$</strong>. This creates an artificial <strong>information explosion</strong>: each player receives $N-1$ updates per match ($3$ full games' worth of statistical evidence for a 4-player game, and $2$ for a 3-player game). But given the TTA context, a four player game is clearly not the same as 6 independant 1vs1 games. The result of A vs B and A vs C are correlated events. Thus we implement:
  </p>

  <h4 class="faq-subhead">
    The Fractional Weighting Rule: $w = \frac{1}{N-1}$
  </h4>
  <p>
    To restore statistical honesty, <a class="faq-link" href="https://www.remi-coulom.fr/WHR/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Coulom's fractional variance weighting</a> scales the match weight according to participant count:
  </p>

  <div class="faq-formula">

$$
w = \frac{1}{N - 1} \implies w_{2\text{p}} = 1.000, \quad w_{3\text{p}} = 0.500, \quad w_{4\text{p}} = 0.333
$$

</div>

  <p>
    This fractional weight scales <strong>both</strong> the rating step discrepancy ($\Delta \mu$) and the variance accumulation ($v^{-1}$):
  </p>

  <div class="faq-formula">

$$
v^{-1} = \sum_{j} w \cdot g(\phi_j)^2 E_j (1 - E_j), \quad \Delta \text{sum} = \sum_{j} w \cdot g(\phi_j) (s_j - E_j)
$$

</div>
          </div>

</div>

        <div class="faq-card faq-card-edge">
  <h3 class="c-yellow" >Does fractional weighting effectively devalue multiplayer matches in the leaderboard?</h3>
  <div>

      <p>
    This is a common intuitive concern among multiplayer competitors: <em>&ldquo;If my 4-player win only counts at weight 0.333, does the system value 4-player games as one-third of a duel? Am I being penalized for playing multiplayer?&rdquo;</em>
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>My answer is - maybe?</strong> The distinction lies between <strong>individual player evidence</strong> versus <strong>total table information</strong>:
  </p>

  <ul class="faq-list">
    <li>
<span><strong>For an Individual Competitor:</strong> In a 4-player game, you play against $3$ opponents ($N-1$). Multiplying each pairwise comparison by $\frac{1}{3}$ yields:</span>
<div class="faq-formula">

$$
(N - 1) \times \frac{1}{N - 1} = 3 \times \frac{1}{3} = 1.0 \text{ unit of evidence per player}
$$

</div>
<span>You gain <strong>exactly 1.0 match worth of statistical certainty</strong> from finishing a 4-player game—the exact same amount of certainty as completing a 1v1 duel! A 4-player victory awards the winner full credit for winning one complete contest.</span>
</li>
    <li>
      <strong>The Alternative Is Unfair Inflation:</strong> If we did <em>not</em> scale by $\frac{1}{N-1}$, a competitor who played 100 4-player games would receive $300$ matches of certainty reduction, artificially collapsing their $\mathrm{RD}$ three times faster than a duel player who played 100 1v1s.
    </li>
    <li>
<span><strong>Table-Level Information Conservation:</strong> Across all 4 participants, a 4-player game generates $\binom{4}{2} = 6$ pairs. At weight $1/3$, the total information generated across the entire table is:</span>
<div class="faq-formula">

$$
I_{\text{table}} = \binom{N}{2} \times \frac{1}{N-1} = \frac{N(N-1)}{2} \times \frac{1}{N-1} = \frac{N}{2} = 2.0 \text{ units}
$$

</div>
<span>A 4-player game generates <strong>twice as much total evidence</strong> as a 1v1 duel ($2.0$ vs $1.0$), partitioned evenly across the 4 competitors.</span>
</li>
  </ul>
